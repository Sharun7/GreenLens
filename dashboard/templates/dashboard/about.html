🔬 Overview

GreenLens is an open-source climate risk intelligence platform designed to bring transparency and scientific rigor to the green bond market. By combining satellite imagery from NASA and ESA with machine learning models, we provide independent verification of climate claims and quantitative risk assessment.

  • Physical Climate Risk Scoring (PCRS): Quantify exposure to drought, flood, heat — plus carbon, policy and transition risk
  • Pricing Analysis: Detect mispriced bonds using quantitative regression models
  • Greenwash Detection: Verify land-use claims using satellite imagery and NDVI analysis

🤖 PCRS Model — All Parameters & Features

The Physical Climate Risk Score (0–100) is computed by an XGBoost ensemble model trained on 20 features across 4 categories:

🌊 Primary Hazard Inputs (World Bank API)
  • Flood Risk Index — normalised 0–1 per project GPS coordinate
  • Heat Stress Index — normalised 0–1 per GPS coordinate
  • Drought Severity (SPEI) — Standardised Precipitation-Evapotranspiration Index
🌍 Extended Risk Inputs (Multi-source)
  • Carbon Intensity Score — EDGAR v8.0 CO₂ per GDP (60+ countries)
  • Policy Risk Score — World Bank Governance Indicators (Rule of Law + Gov. Effectiveness)
  • Transition Risk Score — Climate Action Tracker NDC + IEA fossil fuel dependency
⚙️ Derived / Engineered Features
  • Composite Hazard = Flood×0.40 + Heat×0.35 + Drought×0.25
  • Maturity Exposure = log(maturity_years) × composite_hazard
  • Hazard × Vulnerability = composite_hazard × category_vulnerability
  • Coastal Flood Interaction = is_coastal × flood_risk_index
  • Climate Trend — IPCC AR6 ordinal (1=low warming, 5=very high)
  • Is Coastal — proximity to coastline within 200km
📋 Bond Characteristics + Output
  • Project Category — Solar, Wind, Water, Transport, Building, Reforestation (one-hot encoded)
  • Category Vulnerability — per-category base risk (Water: 0.70, Transport: 0.60...)
  • Bond Maturity Years — longer maturity = higher cumulative climate exposure
  • OUTPUT: PCRS Score 0–100 with SHAP decomposition per feature

🛰️ How Greenwash Detection Works — 3-Step Pipeline

Step 1 — NDVI Change Detection

Sentinel-2 imagery is fetched 12 months before bond issuance and 12 months after claimed project completion. NDVI (vegetation index) is compared. A reforestation bond must show increasing vegetation — if it shows bare soil, a flag is raised.

Step 2 — CNN Land-Use Classification

A fine-tuned ResNet-18 CNN classifies the satellite image patch into 6 categories: Solar Farm, Wind Farm, Forest, Water Body, Urban, or Bare Land. A solar bond whose site is classified as "Dense Forest" is automatically flagged.

Step 3 — Consistency Check

The claimed project type (from bond prospectus) is compared against the CNN's observed land use. Any mismatch triggers a greenwash flag with a confidence score. Company data is never trusted — only satellite pixels.

PCRS Model (Physical Climate Risk Score)

The flagship PCRS model is designed to intuitively score infrastructure risk from 0 (Safe) to 100 (High Risk). We use an XGBoost regression algorithm driven by 25+ real-time environmental features, breaking risk down into simple metrics:

  • Geographic precision: Exact project coordinates across different climate zones.
  • Climate hazards: Historical and projected drought frequency, flood exposure, extreme heat indices.
  • Market alignment: Bond maturity and project lifespan mismatch with worsening climate events.

Explainability with SHAP

Every PCRS score includes SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values that break down exactly which factors contributed to the risk assessment. This provides transparency and allows users to understand the "why" behind each score.

Satellite Verification

The greenwash detection module uses a fine-tuned ResNet-18 CNN to classify satellite image patches into 6 land-use categories. NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index) change detection compares pre- and post-project satellite imagery to verify reforestation and green infrastructure claims.

⚠️ Limitations & Known Biases — Full Transparency

Academic integrity demands honest disclosure. These are the known limitations of GreenLens as a research prototype. Investors must understand these before using any output.

1. Data Gaps

📍 Bond Location — Most Critical

Most bonds use country centroid or city-level geocoding, not exact project GPS. "India Solar Bond" plots at India's centre — actual project in Rajasthan vs Kerala has completely different climate risk. PCRS accuracy is directly affected. Each bond displays a location_confidence tag (exact / city / country).

🛰️ Pre-2015 Satellite Gap

ESA Sentinel-2 launched June 2015. Dataset includes bonds from 2006. 2006–2015 bonds cannot be satellite-verified. These bonds are flagged as "Satellite Unavailable" in the greenwash module.

📈 Yahoo Finance Gaps

Emerging market sovereign bonds (Nigeria, Bangladesh, Pakistan) are not listed on Yahoo Finance. These bonds use synthetic spread estimates — not real market data. Pricing gap analysis for these bonds is lower confidence.

2. Emerging Markets Coverage

⚠️ Systematic bias: Climate risk is highest in emerging markets (Bangladesh, Nigeria, Pakistan), but data coverage is weakest there. High-risk regions are under-represented in the dataset.

3. Model Bias — 3 Identified Types

🌍 Geographic Bias (PCRS)

Training data is Europe-heavy. European bonds have more complete disclosures, so the XGBoost model has learned European bond patterns better. Impact: European bonds scored more accurately. Emerging market bonds may have lower prediction confidence.

🔄 Synthetic Label Bias (PCRS)

PCRS model is trained on synthetic hazard-derived labels, not real bond default events. The model is partially circular — hazard data is both input and the basis for target generation. "Does high PCRS actually predict real-world bond problems?" — not yet validated.

🛰️ CNN Classifier Bias (EuroSAT)

ResNet-18 trained on EuroSAT — primarily European landscapes. Tropical forests, South Asian farmland, African savanna are under-represented. Impact: Kerala reforestation or Nigeria solar projects may misclassify; European solar farms classify accurately.
